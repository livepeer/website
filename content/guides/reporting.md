Everything on the roadmap is owned, and owning it means three things: the work is tracked where anyone can check it, its owner posts an update every month, and it closes with a retrospective.

## Track the work where anyone can check it

Keep a GitHub project board with every active task and issue on it. Milestones are the delivery checkpoints — the phases of the proposal, its deliverables, or quarterly checkpoints — each with a due date, and every issue belongs to one. Keep task status current as things move.

Put the board in the commitment's **Links** on the roadmap, beside the proposal thread and the repo. That is where a claim on the record gets checked.

## Post an update every month

At the end of each month, or when a milestone lands, whichever comes first, as a reply in the proposal's thread on [the forum](https://forum.livepeer.org). Keep it short and easy to scan. Tie every claim to something a reader can open — a pull request, a repository, documentation, a deployment, a demo — and map it to the proposal's milestones. Outcomes, not narrative.

An update carries:

- Project and update number
- Period covered
- Status: **On track**, **At risk** (say what could push it out) or **Off track** (say what changes)
- Summary, one or two sentences, on outcomes
- Completed deliverables, grouped by milestone, each with its link
- What is planned for the next update, and when it is due

On the roadmap: one row in **Roadmap updates** the day you post — the summary as its one line, the status as its Health, the forum post as its Link. The roadmap shows the newest update's health beside the card and the changelog rolls the month up. After six weeks with nothing posted the card reads _No update_, whatever the last one said.

## Close with a retrospective

When the scoped work is done, the commitment owes one more post in the same thread, written while the context is fresh. Factual and about outcomes: what was committed, what was delivered, and the gap between them. Any structure works as long as it covers:

- Introduction — the work in brief, and its scope
- Commitments delivered — with their artifacts, and what was learned making them
- Delivered beyond the commitment — why it was taken on, and what it added
- Commitments not delivered — why, and what to do about it next time
- Impact — what changed for the network, with evidence
- Key learnings — what should shape the next proposal
- Conclusion and what is next — follow-up work, handoffs, recommended next steps

On the roadmap: a row in **Roadmap updates** with Kind set to Retrospective, its one line as the summary and the forum post as its Link. A shipped record leads with its retrospective; until one is posted, the card says _No retrospective_.
